Risk management: non-negotiable foundations

Risk management cannot make a strategy profitable, but it can help limit damage when a trade fails. There is no guaranteed win rate or guaranteed return.

Beginner principle: learn the process on a demo account. When moving to live trading, use only money you can afford to lose and keep risk small enough that a normal losing streak does not threaten your finances.

Before each trade

  1. Identify the setup and the price level that invalidates it.
  2. Place the stop-loss at the technical invalidation point, not where the desired loss amount happens to be.
  3. Calculate the position size using the instrument's contract specifications and account currency.
  4. Account for spread, commissions, swaps, slippage, and possible gaps.
  5. Set daily loss and trade limits before the session. Stop when a limit is reached.
  6. Do not increase size to recover losses; do not move a stop farther away to avoid being wrong.

Simple cash-risk example

If a hypothetical account is $100 and a trader chooses 0.5% planned risk, the planned cash risk is $0.50. This does not mean a $0.50 loss is guaranteed: contract value, costs, and execution can change the result. If the broker's minimum position size makes that risk impossible, skip the trade or keep practicing on demo.

Why gold needs special care

XAUUSD contract sizes, tick values, minimum lots, and margin requirements vary by broker and account type. Never assume a price move translates to a fixed dollar profit without checking your broker's specifications.
